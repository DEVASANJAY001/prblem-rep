Module 1 · Chapter 1

Foundations: What Is a Problem?

Most builders start with a solution and go looking for a problem. This chapter gives you the words to tell the two apart.

In this chapter
The difference between a problem, an idea and a solution · The five parts of a good problem statement · A fill-in template · Practice

1.1 Problem, idea, solution

A problem is a situation where someone's goal is blocked. An idea is a guess about what might help. A solution is something that actually removes the block. People mix them up because ideas arrive faster than understanding.

ProblemClinic staff lose part of every week rebuilding the doctor schedule by hand.
IdeaMaybe a scheduling app could fix it.
SolutionA tool that rebuilds the schedule after a no-show, tested with real staff.

The solution in disguise

"The problem is that we lack an app" is not a problem. It is a solution wearing a problem's clothes. Ask what the app would let someone do that they cannot do today.

1.2 A good problem statement

Good statements are specific enough that two people would picture the same situation. Cover five things:

Who faces it. A specific role, not "everyone".
What happens. Behaviour you could observe.
When, and how often. Recurring problems matter most.
What it costs. Time, money, risk or stress.
What they do today. The current workaround.

1.3 The template

[Role] at [type of workplace] struggle to [goal] because [blocker]. It happens [how often] and costs [time, money or risk]. Today they [workaround].

Weak: "Hospitals need better software."
Stronger: "Clinic managers at small clinics struggle to keep the doctor schedule full because no-shows arrive without warning. It happens [how often] and costs [hours per week]. Today they rebuild the schedule by hand in a spreadsheet."

1.4 Practice

Exercise. Pick five frustrations from your own week. Rewrite each one using the template above. Cross out any that name a solution.

Check yourself

1. Is "we need a mobile app for our shop" a problem statement?

No. It names a solution. Ask what the shop owner cannot do today.

2. Which of the five parts is missing: "Nurses struggle with handover notes"?

How often it happens, what it costs and what they do today are all missing.
